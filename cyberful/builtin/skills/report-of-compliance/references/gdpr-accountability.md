# GDPR accountability reporting profile

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 does not prescribe one universal “Report on Compliance” form. Use the profile to create a **GDPR Accountability and Compliance Evidence Report** that helps a controller or processor demonstrate its documented decisions, measures, evidence gaps, and review boundaries.

Establish whether the entity acts as controller, joint controller, processor, representative, or a combination for each processing context. Record the relevant Member State or cross-border context and route unresolved legal interpretation to the DPO or qualified counsel. Do not convert a technical assessment into a legal opinion, supervisory-authority approval, certification under Articles 42–43, or a statement that every processing operation complies.

The profile covers accountability and principles; lawfulness and special-category conditions; transparency and data-subject rights; privacy by design and default; controller, processor and vendor governance; records of processing; security and testing; breach handling; DPIA and prior consultation; DPO governance; transfers; and codes or certification evidence. It is a reporting index, not a substitute for the underlying record of processing activities, DPIA, transfer assessment, processor agreement, breach record, policy, test, or audit.

Keep personal data out of the compiler input. Point to access-controlled and retention-governed evidence through stable references and digests. Describe data categories and processing purposes at the minimum granularity needed for review, and explicitly record withheld or unavailable evidence.

Official source: [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). The accountability principle appears in Article 5(2); Articles 24, 30, 32, and 35 establish important demonstrability, record, security, and impact-assessment obligations. Use current EDPB and competent supervisory-authority guidance when interpretation depends on context.
